ADMISSIONS PORTAL & TICKETING

GO-ARAL: supporting
1,000 applicants a year.

GO-ARAL is the application and ticketing portal at the National Graduate Office for the Health Sciences (NGOHS), University of the Philippines Manila — built by Ruther Bergonia alongside the DRMS admissions records system, supporting roughly 1,000 graduate applicants annually and around 200 support requests monthly.

Role Senior ICT Assistant
Client NGOHS · UP Manila
Focus Portal / Ticketing / RBAC
Period 2021 — 2025

The problem

NGOHS coordinates graduate admissions for the health sciences at UP Manila — a process that concentrates roughly a thousand applicants into recurring admission cycles, each generating applications, records, and a steady stream of support questions.

Handling that volume without dedicated systems means records living in ad-hoc files and support arriving through unstructured channels, with no way to track what has been answered, resolved, or dropped.

What was built

Two systems, designed to work together:

GO-ARAL

The applicant-facing application and ticketing portal — where graduate applicants apply and where their support requests become tracked tickets instead of lost emails.

DRMS

The records system for graduate admissions — the office's internal source of truth for applicant records.

Ruther architected the APIs, database schemas, reporting systems, and role-based access control behind both, and led deployment to a VPS, staff training, and post-deployment support.

Outcome

The systems support roughly 1,000 graduate applicants annually and around 200 support requests monthly — with admissions records, reporting, and applicant support running through structured, role-controlled software instead of manual channels.

Lessons carried forward

GO-ARAL and DRMS were where the pattern that now defines Ruther's institutional work took shape: architect the APIs and schemas first, treat access control as a first-class design concern, own the deployment, and stay through training and post-deployment support. That same pattern later scaled up into SURI, the university-wide research information system.
